## Post-run diagnostic review (not a replacement score)

The table above is strict string/field matching. Inspection of the raw summaries and answers reveals two different kinds of failure; these observations were made after the run, are agent-reviewed rather than independently human-reviewed, and do not change any scores.

- Codex's goal record retained the privacy requirement and answered `注册页隐私提示` instead of the label `隐私提示`. This is a qualified name for the same object, not evidence that the constraint was forgotten.
- Gemma's two `superseded-color` compressed conditions retained the Article page, but answered `文章` instead of `文章页`. Its `design-switch` goal record retained the privacy notice but answered `隐私声明` instead of `隐私提示`. These name/translation differences should not be treated as established object-selection errors. Future datasets need canonical host object IDs or independently reviewed equivalence rules frozen before evaluation.
- Gemma's ordinary `stale-verification` summary correctly retained revision 5 and invalidated revision-4 verification. Its answer used `作者信息` instead of `作者元数据` and lowercased `Report.jsx` to `report.jsx`. These are precision/canonical-name failures; they do not show reuse of stale verification. Case sensitivity can matter for portable file identity, but this pilot did not execute those answers as paths.
- Qwen's ordinary `superseded-color` summary omitted the Article-page preservation constraint; its reader answered `笔记页`. Its ordinary `design-switch` summary omitted `Signup.jsx@r2` and the desktop/mobile results, and the reader mixed in `Landing.jsx@r9` and required both checks again. These omissions are visible in the recorded compression outputs.
- Qwen's `design-switch` goal record retained the privacy requirement and current revision, but its reader answered the overly broad `注册页` as the preserved object. Keeping information in a record is not enough to ensure the reader uses it precisely.
- Gemma's `revoked-preservation` goal record dropped both successful checks and the no-pending-work fact, substituting a task about old footer notes. Its reader unnecessarily chose `inspect` despite an empty check list. The ordinary summary and full history passed this case.
- Gemma's ordinary `design-switch` summary omitted the revision number and preview outcomes. Its reader returned `Signup.jsx` without a revision and required both checks; the goal record preserved the revision and failed-mobile-check distinction.

Thus the pilot gives mixed evidence: goal-oriented summaries preserved useful facts in some cases and lost authoritative completion evidence in another. It does not establish an overall accuracy advantage. A practical follow-up is to preserve current file identity, revision and verification results directly from host/tool state, while testing model compression of user goals and preferences separately.
